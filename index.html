About Me

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Institute of Urban Environment, Chinese Academy of Sciences, working at the intersection of macroecology, aquatic ecology, pollution ecology, and ecological data science. My research explores how environmental change and emerging pollutants influence biodiversity, species traits, and ecological functioning across aquatic and semi-aquatic ecosystems.

My work combines ecological field observations with large-scale ecological datasets, trait-based approaches, and quantitative modelling to understand how organisms respond to environmental pressures across species, communities, and ecosystems.

Current Research

A major component of my current research examines the ecology and movement of microplastics and other emerging pollutants across aquatic and terrestrial food webs. I use ecological datasets, species traits, and statistical modelling to investigate patterns of pollutant exposure and vulnerability among organisms.

More broadly, I am interested in understanding how environmental change, human pressures, and ecological processes interact to influence biodiversity and ecosystem functioning in the Anthropocene.

Alongside research, I am involved in teaching, mentoring, scientific writing, and ecological data analysis, supporting students and early-career researchers in developing quantitative and research skills.

PhD Research

My doctoral research investigated the ecological functioning of riverine macroinvertebrate communities across different mesohabitats in the Wei River Basin, Shaanxi Province, Northwest China. The work examined how environmental drivers and river geomorphology influence macroinvertebrate communities and ecosystem functioning, integrating field ecology with quantitative approaches including piecewise structural equation modelling.
